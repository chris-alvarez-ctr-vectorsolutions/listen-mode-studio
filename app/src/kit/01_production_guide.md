# Production guide

The rules every module script follows. Read this before any stage.

**The voice example (02_voice_example.md) outranks this guide.** If a rule here seems to push a script away from how that example sounds, follow the example. Example wording in this guide shows a move; never reuse it as a line.

## 1. The format

A friendly expert interview. A host asks what a listener would ask, and an experienced safety pro answers from what he's seen. It is not a debate, a lecture, or a radio drama.

A module is a set of **parts**. The LMS assembles each learner's listen from those parts, based on a short quiz before the listen and checks after it.

Every listen: **open**, then the topic parts in a fixed order with a sting between them, then the **close**. There are no bridges: each part's first lines do the job of moving to a new topic. The simulation follows, outside the audio. The audio never hands off to it.

## 2. The hosts

**Dana (host)**
- Asks the question the listener is thinking, including the obvious one.
- Genuinely doesn't know the answer. She is curious, not a straight man.
- Can admit she has taken the shortcut herself.
- Paraphrases a point at most once per part, and only when she adds something.

**Ray (expert)**
- Long career in workplace safety, at a few different companies. Describe employers generically ("a big industrial manufacturer"). Never name a real company.
- Speaks from experience: "I always tell teams...", "I've seen..."
- Carries most of the words, roughly two-thirds.
- Plain, warm, specific. Gives steps as numbered lists when there is a procedure.
- Offers his background only when Dana asks about it, or as a short aside that backs up a point he's making. Never volunteers it, and never in the open.
- His background is characterization only. It never carries a fact.

**Characters (for example, Jacob)**
- Dramatized people who tell one story in short, concrete sentences.
- Appear only in their own part. Never mentioned in the open or another part.
- They never explain the lesson. The host carries any facts around them.

## 3. Part types

| Label | What it is | How it plays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Everyone (Universal) | A topic every learner hears | Always in the listen |
| Skippable (Test-out) | A topic a learner can test out of | Cut when the quiz shows they know it |
| Normal (Base) | The default version of a topic | Plays unless swapped |
| Harder (Test-up) | A harder scene with the same facts | **Replaces** Normal for learners who know it but can't skip it |
| Add-on (Reinforced) | Extra story | **Inserted** into Normal, just before that part's ending, for learners who aren't convinced yet |
| Review set (Remedial) | A few fact cards and a retry question | Shown on screen after the checks for a missed Know question. Not audio, and never part of the listen. |

**The master script** is the longest path: every topic with every add-on inserted. Every other listen is the master with parts removed or swapped.

## 4. Modular rules

1. Every part stands alone. No references to other parts: no "again," "this time," "like we said."
2. Topic order is fixed. Each position always covers the same subject.
3. The open may only promise content from positions that play in every listen. It never promises a skippable topic.
4. No bridges. Each part's first exchange names its topic in plain words, so it works whatever played before it.
5. Harder versions stand alone. The learner never heard Normal.
6. Add-ons sit just before the part's ending line and pick up mid-story without re-introducing anyone.
7. Each part's ending is its own segment, so an add-on can slot in front of it.
8. Review sets stand alone. Each card rests on a ledger claim and never refers to the listen, the hosts or a character.

## 5. Fact rules (these override everything else)

- Facts come only from the signed claims ledger. Rephrase, round down or simplify freely. Never add to a claim, strengthen it, generalize it, or combine two into a new one.
- Examples (like a list of objects that count as sharps) must appear in the source content or the ledger.
- A line that needs a missing fact: write around it and list the gap. A new fact goes to the SME, not into the script.
- Flagged claims are used only as the ledger allows, marked, and never resolved in the script.
- Characters' and hosts' personal moments never carry a fact, statistic, injury, exposure or outcome.

## 6. Learning rules

- Each part serves one objective and carries that objective's required claims.
- Know parts restate the core rule once, near the end.
- Feel parts change what the listener believes through story or evidence. They don't instruct.
- Perceive (norm) parts show what people do. They never tell the listener what to do.
- A judgment hold is optional: at most one per Know part. The host poses the question, the cue sheet holds 3 seconds, and the expert answers.

## 7. Craft rules

- **The open** follows the shape of the voice example's cold open: one or two plain lines naming today's topic (only content every learner hears), the hosts' names, and a short line to start. No scene, no characters, no host backgrounds. Under 60 words.
- **Every setup needs its payoff in the same part.** If a line sets up a moment (a bag being lifted, a blade on a bench), the part must deliver what happens next: the stick, the near miss, the choice. Never tell the listener to hold onto a picture.
- **No speaker has more than two lines in a row.** The exception is a character telling their own story.
- At least one concrete, visual detail per part (the blade on the bench, not "improper disposal").
- Plain words, contractions, few idioms. Many listeners speak English as a second language.
- **Answers are full spoken sentences, not notes.** Ray talks the way people answer a question out loud: "Pretty much my entire working life. I've worked in plants, warehouses, and even a couple of labs." Never telegraphic fragments like "Plants, warehouses, a couple of labs."
- **Both hosts improvise a little.** Light fillers and softeners ("Uhh," "pretty much," "honestly," "I mean") appear once or twice per part per speaker. Not in every line.
- **Every question connects to the answer before it.** Dana builds on what Ray just said ("So if you walked into a new company today...") instead of jumping to a new question cold.
- **Nothing is referred to before it's introduced.** Never "the shortcut" until someone has said what shortcut. Dana can introduce a topic with an invented bit of shared history: "We talked a bit before the show, and you mentioned shortcuts."
- **Scenarios are framed and flow.** Ray sets up a hypothetical out loud ("Let's say...") and links the steps with "but," "so" and "and," in one or two sentences, rather than a string of short separate statements.
- Fragments are for reactions only ("Right." "Wait, really?").
- Short reactions ("Mm-hm." "Right." "Wait, really?") are their own lines.
- At most one light moment per part. Humor comes from the situation, not quips.
- Each part uses one way in and one way out from the lists below. None repeats within a listen. These describe moves; write fresh wording every time.

**Ways in**
- Dana asks the question a listener would ask.
- Dana brings up something Ray mentioned before the show.
- Ray describes something he saw on the floor, with its outcome.
- Dana admits she's taken the shortcut herself.
- A surprising fact, from the claims ledger only.
- A plain one-line setup of the topic.
- The tease: Ray hints, Dana asks, Ray names it.

**Ways out**
- Ray's rule of thumb, in his own words. Once per listen.
- Dana says what she'd do differently now.
- A callback to the moment the part opened with.
- A short acknowledgment that closes the part cleanly.
- A question left with the listener.
- Dana's short sign-off to Ray. Once per listen.

## 8. Length

| Asset | Words | About |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | 25 to 60 | 10 to 25 seconds |
| Topic part, Normal or Harder | 200 to 280 | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| Add-on | 40 to 100 | 20 to 45 seconds |
| Close | 20 to 50 | 10 to 20 seconds |
| Review set (per Know topic) | 3 to 5 cards, each 1 to 2 sentences on the back | read on screen |

Runtime = words / 140, plus holds, plus about 3 seconds per sting.

## 9. Render conventions (ElevenLabs)

- Model: Eleven v4 (same tag handling as v3). Start speed at 0.95.
- Render text holds spoken words, bracketed delivery tags and punctuation only. SFX, beds, holds and stings live on the cue sheet.
- Speaker labels (DANA:, RAY:) map lines to voices. Send only the text after the colon.
- Tags: at most two to four per part, only where the meaning changes. Keep to each voice's palette.
  - Ray: [dry] [matter-of-fact] [thoughtful] [warmly] [laughs] [quietly]
  - Dana: [playful] [skeptical] [curious] [surprised] [sighs] [laughs] [softly] [quietly]
  - Characters: [tired] [flatly] [quietly]
- Emphasis: CAPS on one or two words per part. No SSML.
- Short pauses: an ellipsis or [pause]. Anything 2 seconds or longer is a hold on the cue sheet.
- Spell out as spoken: numbers ("more than nine out of ten"), initialisms (H-I-V, P-P-E, U-S).
- Split each part into render segments at every hold, add-on seam and ending.

## 10. Editing in ElevenLabs Studio

Delivery gets fixed in Studio. Content gets fixed in the script.

| Problem | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Mispronounced word or glitch | Regenerate the words in Studio; add to the pronunciation dictionary |
| Flat or wrong emotion | Change the tag or punctuation in Studio, regenerate, copy the change back to the script |
| Pause or SFX timing | Studio timeline, or leave it to the assembly engine |
| The words themselves are wrong | Back to the drafting stage, so the new words pass the validity pass |
