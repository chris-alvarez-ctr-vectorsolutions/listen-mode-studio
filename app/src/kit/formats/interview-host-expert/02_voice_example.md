# Voice example

Written by hand by Chris Alvarez. This is a sample of register: how the hosts sound. It is one good script, not the standard for how a script is shaped. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this script's sequence of moves, its questions or its lines.

The cold open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Cold open (fixed shape)

DANA: Today, on the show we're talking about sharps: what they are, how to handle them, and the dangers of not following procedure.
DANA: I'm Dana...
RAY: And I'm Ray.
DANA: Let's get into it.

## Host intro (only when the expert has a background; same asset as the open)

DANA: Ray, you spent a long time in commercial kitchens before this, didn't you?
RAY: Twenty-some years, mostly on the line, then training new cooks.
DANA: Then you've seen every way a sandwich can go wrong.
RAY: Most of them, yes.
DANA: Good. Let's start.

## Close (fixed shape)

DANA: Well, that's it for today. Thank you for listening!
RAY: Keep learning, stay safe, and we'll be back soon.

## Register sample

An excerpt from the first topic part. Read it for how the hosts talk, not for what happens in it.

DANA: Sharps. Ok. So what is a sharp?
RAY: So, usually when people think of a sharp, they think of a needle, but a sharp can be anything that is capable of piercing the skin: nails, knives, blades, broken glass, metal... you get the idea.
DANA: Ah so that sounds like the hard part: remembering that it's not just needles, it's anything that could puncture the skin.
RAY: Exactly. And keep in mind you can still find needles in the workplace. Some people need to manage their diabetes or allergies with syringes.
DANA: Let's look why sharps are so dangerous. What is one of the most common, and perhaps most preventable, shortcuts workers take that result in an injury?
RAY: One of the most common shortcuts is simply leaving a blade in the open. Every shift there's someone who leaves a box-cutter blade on a bench thinking "I'll take care of it in a minute." But, a minute is all it takes for an injury.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- Plain, warm words. Mostly full spoken sentences with some varied rhythm, never bare notes.
- Conversational connectors that sound like speech: "So," "Ah," "Exactly," "you get the idea."
- Dana asks what a listener would ask, and builds each question on the answer before it.
- The expert speaks from experience and in the words people actually use on the job.
- A concrete detail does the work an abstract word would otherwise do.
- Light improvisation, once or twice per part per speaker, not in every line.

## Not required

This script happens to use certain moves: a tease with a one-word reveal, Dana paraphrasing the key insight, Ray giving four numbered steps, a "final thoughts" question. They are options, not a template. A part may use none of them. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Beat sheet format

The beats for the first topic part, written before the dialogue. This shows the format of a beat sheet only.

- Introduce or reinforce the concept of sharps: basic definition; not just needles (diabetes, allergies).
- Common shortcuts.
- The right approach.
- Final points.

## The open: what to avoid

A generated cold open that missed. The shape of the open is fixed, so these are the ways it went wrong.

**Missed**

DANA: End of a shift. Somebody grabs a full trash bag by the knot and lifts it off the floor.
DANA: That's the whole moment. Hold onto that picture.
DANA: Today is for anyone on a production floor who works around used blades and needles.
DANA: We'll look at a hazard you can walk right past. Why a container does more than a pair of gloves. And what most people actually do.
DANA: You'll also hear from Jacob. He's a character we made up, based on a situation from this training.
DANA: I'm Dana.
RAY: And I'm Ray. I've worked in safety my whole career, at a few different companies.
DANA: Let's get into it.

**What went wrong**
- It opens on an image with no payoff. The setup is never delivered.
- Dana talks for six lines straight. That's a monologue, not a conversation.
- It mentions a character. Characters appear only in their own part.
- Ray volunteers his background outside the host intro. After the intro, he shares it only as an aside that supports a point.

**Approved (generated, then kept as written)**

DANA: Our topic today is sharps. The hazards that are easy to miss, why a container beats gloves, and what people on the floor really do.
DANA: I'm Dana.
RAY: And I'm Ray.
DANA: Here we go.

## Principles from review

These were caught in review of generated topic parts. Each is a rule, not a line to copy.

- **Answers are full spoken sentences with a little improvisation.** A bare list of fragments reads as notes, not speech.
- **Each question builds on the answer before it.** A new question that ignores the last answer sounds like a form.
- **Never refer to something the listener hasn't heard yet.** If a host needs shared history, they say so out loud.
- **Frame a scenario, then let it flow as one connected thought.** A string of short separate statements breaks it up.

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure the claims ledger doesn't hold,** however natural it sounds in a host's mouth.
- **Check every example list against the source content** before using it.
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.** A topic that can test out is not promised.
- **Stock host lines** ("Any final thoughts?", "Solid advice") are fine once per listen, not in every part.
