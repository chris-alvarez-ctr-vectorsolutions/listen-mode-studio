## D. Uncanny-valley lint

| Check | Starting target |
| --- | --- |
| Ray's share of words in an expert part | About 55 to 60 percent |
| Restatements (a point repeated in the same or next turn, or a figure in a second form) | None |
| Echo exchanges and repeated question/answer frames ("Even X?" / "Even X.") | Once per listen |
| Each way in and way out | Used once per listen |
| "Solid advice," "Any final thoughts?" and other stock host lines | Once per listen each |
| Turn length | Mixed: fragments, short and long turns. Not uniform |
| Concrete, visual details per part | At least one |
| Light or funny moments per part | One at most |
| CAPS emphasis per part | One or two words |

## E. Conversation

- [ ] The open starts on the sharpest line from content every learner hears (no agenda read-out, no host background) and stays under 60 words.
- [ ] Every setup has its payoff in the same part. No "hold onto that picture."
- [ ] A host intro exists only if the cast guide gives the expert a background, and is absent otherwise. When present: it follows the open in the same asset, runs 40 to 80 words, and has the host state one specific fact from the background text and ask a short question that invites a reaction, with the expert answering in one or two sentences in the first person: he answers, then gives a view or feeling that builds on the background, without parroting her sentence back or listing the background as she did, and without adding any career fact beyond it. The expert is never introduced as a guest, and no background or role is invented. It ends on a line tying his experience to the episode's subject, not a generic hand-off ("let's get going"). It names and promises no topic part, and the open's closing line promises nothing (no "a quick hello"). The background is not repeated in other parts, except as a short aside backing a point.
- [ ] No characters are mentioned outside their own part.
- [ ] No example wording from the production guide, lint checklist or pattern library appears in the script.
- [ ] No line repeats a line from the voice example, and no part follows its sequence of moves (the tease with a one-word reveal, Dana's paraphrase, four numbered steps, a "final thoughts" question) unless that part's beat sheet chose those moves.
- [ ] No two parts in a listen share the same overall shape.
- [ ] Answers sound like speech: mostly full sentences with some varied rhythm. No bare lists of notes and no telegraphic fragments with no connective.
- [ ] Every question from Dana connects to Ray's previous answer.
- [ ] Nothing ("the shortcut," "that rule") is referred to before it's been introduced.
- [ ] Every scenario Ray describes is framed ("Let's say...") and flows as one or two linked sentences.
- [ ] Read aloud, every line sounds like something a person would say out loud.
- [ ] An interruption never occurs in a part about harm or inside a character's story, and never cuts off a ledger claim.
- [ ] Dana has turns other than questions (a reaction, a scenario she sets up, what she'd do differently). A wrong assumption she voices is corrected in full in the next turn and is backed by the ledger.
- [ ] No fact, list or statistic inside a character's story. The facts follow the story.
- [ ] No canned personality line.
- [ ] Every expert turn answers a question or invitation the host aimed at him in the turn before. No question is left in the air for him to pick up.
