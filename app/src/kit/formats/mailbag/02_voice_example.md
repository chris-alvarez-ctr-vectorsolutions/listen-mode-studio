# Voice example

This is a sample of register for the mailbag: how the hosts sound. It is a draft sample on a deliberately light topic (the fictional focaccia sandwich module), to be replaced with a hand-written one. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this sample's sequence of moves, its questions or its lines.

The open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Cold open (sharpest line first, then names)

DANA: "Is it really that big a deal if I skip it?" That's a real question we're answering today.
DANA: I'm Dana.
RAY: And I'm Ray. Let's get to them.

## Host intro (only when the expert has a background; same asset as the open)

DANA: Ray, you spent a long time in commercial kitchens before this, didn't you?
RAY: Twenty-some years, mostly on the line, then training new cooks.
DANA: Then you've seen every way a sandwich can go wrong.
RAY: Most of them, yes.
DANA: Good. Let's start.

## Close (a question or a callback, no thank-you)

DANA: Before you go, one to chew on: what would you ask us next?
RAY: Keep asking. We'll see you next time.

## Register sample

An excerpt from a topic part. Read it for how the hosts talk, not for what happens in it.

DANA: A prep cook asks: "My sandwiches look great at the pass, but by the time they get to the table the bread's soggy. Am I doing something wrong?"
RAY: To the prep cook who asked: probably, and it's an easy one to fix. It's almost never the bread.
DANA: Wait, really? I'd have blamed the bread.
RAY: Most people do. It's usually the order the layers go on. Tomato's the wettest thing on the board, so it goes last, and it gets patted dry first.
DANA: So the real question is how to build it, not how to buy it.
RAY: Exactly.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- Plain, warm words. Full spoken sentences, with a few short reactions on their own line.
- The question read as asked, in the asker's own words, introduced by role.
- The answer starts by speaking to the asker, then goes to the question underneath.
- Light improvisation ("honestly," "I mean") once or twice per part per speaker.
- Dana's follow-ups build on what Ray just said.

## Not required

This sample happens to use certain moves: the question read whole, Ray speaking to the asker, the real question underneath, Dana's one-line restatement. They are options, not a template. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Principles from review

- **Answers are full spoken sentences with a little improvisation.**
- **Each follow-up builds on the answer before it.**
- **Never refer to something the listener hasn't heard yet.**

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure the claims ledger doesn't hold,** and never say how many people asked.
- **Never call an invented question real.**
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.**
- **Stock host lines** ("Any final thoughts?", "Solid advice") are fine once per listen, not in every part.
