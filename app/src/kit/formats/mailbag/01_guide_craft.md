## 7. Craft rules

- **The open** is a cold open: the sharpest line in content every learner hears (a line from a story, a striking detail), then the hosts' names and one short line to start. It is not an agenda read-out and does not list what's coming. It does not claim the questions came from real people. Under 60 words. Quoted story lines are allowed here only when that story's part plays in every listen. No host backgrounds.
- **The host intro** exists only when the cast guide gives the expert a background (the text under the expert's name). With no background there is no host intro: the open ends on its short line to start, and the first topic part follows. Never invent a background, a role question or a career for the expert. When there is a background, the host intro follows the open in the same asset, with no sting between them, so it plays for every learner. The expert is a co-host who has already introduced himself, so the host never introduces him as a guest ("you're the practitioner on the show"). The host says the expert's name and states one specific fact taken from the background text in the host's own words, as something she knows about him, and asks a short question that fits it. The expert answers in two or three sentences in his own words, using that background and nothing more, and the host says one short line to move on. 40 to 80 words. It names no topic part and promises nothing, because any topic part can be skipped; the first part's own signpost starts the content. The open's closing line is a plain hand-off that promises nothing, never "a quick hello" or "first, ...". Characterization only: it never carries a fact, and the background is not repeated in other parts except as a short aside that backs up a point.
- **One question per part.** Dana reads it in the asker's own words, context included, introduced by role only ("A line lead asks..."). Never a name, a site or an employer.
- **Where the questions come from.** Use real questions from the seed's learner questions field and the source files (LMS threads, help-desk tickets, SME FAQs), lightly edited for length and with anything identifying removed. If an objective has no real question, write one in a plain learner's voice and introduce it as "a question you might have" or "one you might hear." Never call an invented question real, and never say how many people asked.
- **Answer the question as asked in one line, then the real one.** A narrow question is often a bigger worry. Ray names the bigger worry and answers that too.
- **Facts come only from the claims ledger.** When the ledger doesn't settle a question, Ray says plainly "that depends on your site," points to the procedure, and gives no guess about the fact.
- **Ray starts by speaking to the asker, by role.**
- **The close** may say where to send a question for next time only if the seed says where. It ends on a reflection question or a callback, never a bare sign-off or a thank-you.
- **No speaker has more than two lines in a row.** The question itself is the exception: Dana may read it whole.
- At least one concrete, visual detail per part (the specific thing in the specific place, not an abstract label).
- Plain words, contractions, few idioms. Many listeners speak English as a second language.
- **Answers sound like speech.** Mostly full spoken sentences, but vary the rhythm the way people do: a short fragment, a trailing thought or a quick restart is fine in an answer when a person would say it that way ("Twenty years. Different kinds of places."). Never a bare list of notes, and never telegraphic fragments with no connective. A short reaction rides at the front of the next line, never its own turn.
- **Every follow-up connects to the answer before it.**
- **Nothing is referred to before it's introduced.** Never "that step" until someone has said which step.
- **At most one interruption per part, in the conversational parts only.** When a speaker jumps in, mark it in the text: a dash ending the interrupted line and a dash starting the interrupting one ("So the part people forget is—" / "—the label, right."). Use it only where a real listener would already know where the line is going. Never in a part about harm, never inside a character's own story, and never in a way that cuts off a ledger claim. The interrupted line must still make sense without its ending.
- At most one light moment per part. Humor comes from the situation, not quips.
- Each part uses one way in and one way out from the lists below. None repeats within a listen. These describe moves; write fresh wording every time.

**Ways in**
- Dana reads the question, then says why it stood out.
- Dana reads the question, then adds the one detail that came with it.
- Dana reads the question and says she has wondered the same thing.
- Dana reads the question, and Ray says what he hears underneath it.

**Ways out**
- Ray's rule of thumb, in his own words. Once per listen.
- Dana says what she'd do differently now.
- The answer, said again in plain words.
- A pointer to the procedure for the part the ledger doesn't settle.

## 8. Length

| Asset | Words | About |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Open | 25 to 60 | 10 to 25 seconds |
| Host intro (same asset as the open) | 40 to 80 | 15 to 30 seconds |
| Topic part (Base, Test-up or single) | 200 to 280 | 1:30 to 2:00 |
| Close | 20 to 50 | 10 to 20 seconds |

Runtime = words / 140, plus holds, plus about 3 seconds per sting.

## 9. Render conventions (ElevenLabs)

- Model: Eleven v4 (same tag handling as v3). Start speed at 0.95.
- Render text holds spoken words, bracketed delivery tags and punctuation only. SFX, beds, holds and stings live on the cue sheet.
- Speaker labels (DANA:, RAY:) map lines to voices. Send only the text after the colon.
- Tags: about four to eight per part, only where the meaning or emotion changes, never on consecutive lines. Place a tag just before the line it shapes, or just after a line as a reaction ([sighs], [laughs]). Keep to each voice's palette. Never [quietly], [softly] or a variant on a host, expert or narrator. A tag may be two short descriptive words in that voice's register ([warm, amused], [dry, deadpan]); never staging or sound tags ([music], [standing]).
  - Ray: [dry] [matter-of-fact] [thoughtful] [warmly] [laughs] [amused] [reassuring] [sighs] [patient] [firmly] [concerned] [wry] [slowly] [chuckles] [exhales] [clears throat]
  - Dana: [playful] [skeptical] [curious] [surprised] [sighs] [laughs] [amused] [warmly] [jumping in] [realizing] [impressed] [wry] [unsure] [relieved] [mock-serious] [thinking out loud] [exhales] [clears throat]
- Emphasis: CAPS on one or two words per part. No SSML.
- Short pauses: an ellipsis, a dash or punctuation (never [pause] or SSML break tags). Anything 2 seconds or longer is a hold on the cue sheet.
- Spell out as spoken: numbers ("more than nine out of ten"), initialisms (H-I-V, P-P-E, U-S).
- Split each part into render segments at every hold and at the ending.
