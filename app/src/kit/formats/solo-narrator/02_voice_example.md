# Voice example

This is a sample of register for the solo narrator: how the voice sounds. It is a draft sample on a deliberately trivial topic, to be replaced with a hand-written one. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this sample's sequence of moves, its questions or its lines.

The open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Open (fixed shape)

NARRATOR: Somewhere in your building right now, there's a yellow sign standing on a floor that's already dry. You've walked past it a hundred times.
NARRATOR: So why does it matter? Because the one time it's missing is the one time somebody doesn't see the floor.
NARRATOR: Today we're talking about wet floors: what makes them risky, and what to do about them.

## Close (fixed shape)

NARRATOR: That's it for today. Say what you see, fix what you can, and look out for each other.

## Register sample

An excerpt from a topic part. Read it for how the voice talks, not for what happens in it.

NARRATOR: Here's the idea to hold onto: a wet floor is only dangerous when nobody knows about it.
NARRATOR: First, what counts as a wet floor. You're probably thinking of a spill, and that's part of it. But a floor can also be wet from a mop, from rain on somebody's boots, or from a pipe nobody has noticed yet.
NARRATOR: Which is the hard part.
NARRATOR: Why? Because you don't see a floor the way you see a spill. You see where you're going. So what do you do when you do notice one? You make it visible to the next person, and you do it before you do anything else.
NARRATOR: That's the whole idea again, in plain words. A wet floor is only dangerous when nobody knows about it.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- Plain, warm words. Full spoken sentences, with a short line now and then for emphasis.
- Plain signposts ("First, ...") and one-line pivots on their own.
- Talks to "you," and says what the listener is probably thinking.
- Asks a question to the listener, then answers it.
- The one big idea is stated early and said again at the end.
- Nothing about the narrator. The listener is the subject.

## Not required

This sample happens to use certain moves: an idea stated up front, a signpost, a one-line pivot, a question to the listener, an idea restated at the end. They are options, not a template. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Principles from review

- **Every line is a full spoken sentence,** except the rare fragment kept for emphasis.
- **Nothing is mentioned before it has been introduced.**
- **Frame a scenario, then let it flow as one connected thought.**

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure the claims ledger doesn't hold.**
- **Check every example list against the source content** before using it.
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.**
