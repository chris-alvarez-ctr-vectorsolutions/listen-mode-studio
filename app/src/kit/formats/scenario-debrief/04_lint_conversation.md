## E. Scenario and debrief

- [ ] The open names the topic and promises only content every learner hears, has no story, no character and no host background, and stays under 60 words.
- [ ] The character tells the story in the first person, and Dana only compresses time and place.
- [ ] The part starts wide, then narrows to one place and one person, with a plan and a moment it fell apart.
- [ ] Ray's explanation comes inside the story at the moment it matters, not afterward in a summary.
- [ ] Any graphic detail is preceded by one plain content warning.
- [ ] The part ends on a second person's reaction, not a moral, and nobody states the lesson.
- [ ] Every technical detail traces to the claims ledger. The story invents only people and small details, never a statistic, injury count, exposure level or medical outcome.
- [ ] A debrief that is its own part opens with one line restating the moment.
- [ ] No speaker has more than two lines in a row (the character telling his own story is exempt).
- [ ] The character appears only in his own part.
- [ ] A host intro exists only if the cast guide gives the expert a background, and is absent otherwise. When present: it follows the open in the same asset, runs 40 to 80 words, and has the host state one specific fact from the background text and ask a short question that fits it, with the expert answering in two or three sentences using only that background. The expert is never introduced as a guest, and no background or role is invented. It ends on a line tying his experience to the episode's subject, not a generic hand-off ("let's get going"). It names and promises no topic part, and the open's closing line promises nothing (no "a quick hello"). The background is not repeated in other parts, except as a short aside backing a point.
- [ ] Nobody names the listener's feeling. Small physical details carry it.
- [ ] Every setup has its payoff in the same part. No "hold onto that picture."
- [ ] No example wording from the production guide, lint checklist or pattern library appears in the script.
- [ ] No line repeats a line from the voice example, and no part follows its sequence of moves unless its beat sheet chose them.
- [ ] No two parts in a listen share the same overall shape.
- [ ] Read aloud, every line sounds like something a person would say out loud.
- [ ] At most one interruption per part, marked with dashes, never in a part about harm or inside a character's story, and never cutting off a ledger claim.
- [ ] Nobody asks a character a question, speaks to a character, or waits for an answer. A short story (about 80 words or fewer) may be told by the host or expert instead of a character voice.
- [ ] No standalone one-word reaction turn. Reactions ride on the front of the same speaker's next line.
- [ ] Dana has turns other than questions (a reaction, a scenario she sets up, what she'd do differently). A wrong assumption she voices is corrected in full in the next turn and is backed by the ledger.
- [ ] No fact, list or statistic inside a character's story. The facts follow the story.
- [ ] No "before the show" or "off mic" setup, no host thanking another for explaining, no canned personality line.
- [ ] Every expert turn answers a question or invitation the host aimed at him in the turn before. No question is left in the air for him to pick up.
