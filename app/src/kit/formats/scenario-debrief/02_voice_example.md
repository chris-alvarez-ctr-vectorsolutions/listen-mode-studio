# Voice example

This is a sample of register for a scenario and debrief: how the voices sound. It is a draft sample on a deliberately light topic (the fictional focaccia sandwich module), to be replaced with a hand-written one. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this sample's sequence of moves, its lines or its details.

The open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Cold open (sharpest line first, then names)

DANA: "It looked fine." That's where our story starts.
DANA: I'm Dana.
RAY: And I'm Ray. Let's get into it.

## Host intro (only when the expert has a background; same asset as the open)

DANA: Ray, you spent a long time in commercial kitchens before this, didn't you?
RAY: Twenty-some years, mostly on the line, then training new cooks.
DANA: Then you've seen every way a sandwich can go wrong.
RAY: Most of them, yes.
DANA: Good. Let's start.

## Close (a question or a callback, no thank-you)

DANA: One question for your own counter: what would have made you stop and look twice?
RAY: We'll be back soon.

## Register sample

An excerpt from a topic part. Read it for how the voices talk, not for what happens in it.

DANA: Here's a story about one sandwich that went out looking fine. Lunch rush, a casual cafe, tickets stacked up on the rail.
DANA: You'll hear from Jacob, who works the sandwich line.
JACOB: I built it the way I always do. Cheese, meat, greens, tomato. It looked great at the pass.
JACOB: Then I heard the bell, and I didn't look at it again.
RAY: That's the moment I'd be watching. Not the building, the part after, when it's out of your hands.
JACOB: Table six sent it back. The bread had gone soft on the bottom. I just stood there.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- The character in the first person, in short, concrete sentences, with one small physical detail.
- The host compresses time and place in a sentence or two and never retells.
- The expert explains at the moment it matters, in plain words, tied to what just happened.
- Full spoken sentences, with a few short reactions.
- The host opens with a signpost and introduces the character before the character speaks.
- Nobody names the feeling or states the lesson.

## Not required

This sample happens to use certain moves: a scene-setting line, a first-person run, an expert stepping in mid-story, a closing reaction. They are options, not a template. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Principles from review

- **Short, concrete sentences for the character; full spoken sentences for everyone else.**
- **The explanation comes when it's needed, not afterward.**
- **Never refer to something the listener hasn't heard yet.**

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure, injury or outcome the claims ledger doesn't hold.**
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.** A story is never in the open.
- **No part ends on a moral.**
