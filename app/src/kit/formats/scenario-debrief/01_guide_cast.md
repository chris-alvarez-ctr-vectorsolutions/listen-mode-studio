## 1. The format

A scenario with a debrief. Each part tells one short story about a person at work, and the person tells it in their own words. The host sets the scene and compresses time and place. An experienced practitioner from the industry says what they would notice, and why, at the moment it matters inside the story. It is not a lecture with a story pasted on, and it is not a radio drama with sound effects.

A module is a set of **parts**. The LMS assembles each learner's listen from those parts, based on a short quiz before the listen and checks after it.

Every listen: **open**, then the topic parts in a fixed order with a sting between them, then the **close**. There are no bridges, so each part's first lines do the job of moving to a new topic, and they do it as a spoken signpost that sounds like a person talking, never a label. The host brings the topic up conversationally, in a different way for each part. Moves to choose from, described and never to be copied as wording: turn to a co-speaker with a question that leads into the topic; react to something the previous part left behind, only if it can be said without referring to it; put a small everyday situation in front of the listener and ask what they would do; ask the co-speaker a plain "what is" or "why does" question; or state the plain stake in one sentence. Addressing a co-speaker by name is one option, used at most once per listen, never as a formula like "[name], let's talk about…". The host says what kind of thing comes next (a story, a question, a case) before any of it begins. Never open with a label such as "New topic:" or "Next up:". Any voice the listener hasn't heard yet is introduced by name and role right before it speaks, never mentioned and then cut to. The simulation follows, outside the audio. The audio never hands off to it.

## 2. The cast

**Dana (host)**
- Sets the scene in the first lines: the big picture, then one place and one person. States the plan, then the moment it fell apart, with a time marker.
- Only compresses time and place, in one or two sentences at a time. She never retells what the character just said.
- Introduces each character by name and role, and hands off to them, before the character's first line.
- Says one plain content warning before any graphic detail.
- Genuinely doesn't know the answer when she asks. She is curious, not a straight man.
- Never states the lesson. She names what the story is about, not what to do.

**Jacob (character)**
- A dramatized person who tells one story, in the first person, in short, concrete sentences. Only when the story is longer than a few lines; a short story can be told by the host or expert instead. No one ever asks him a question, speaks to him, or waits for him to answer: his account is a recording, not a live interview.
- Appears only in his own part. Never mentioned in another part. The open may quote them only when their part plays in every listen.
- Tells what he saw, felt and did, with small physical details (a hand that wouldn't stop shaking, a box left half open). He never explains the lesson.
- The people and details in his story are characterization. They never carry a fact, a statistic, an injury count, an exposure level or a medical outcome the claims ledger doesn't hold.

- Explains what he'd notice, and why he'd act, at the moment it matters inside the story, in one or two plain sentences. Never afterward in a summary.
- Speaks from experience: "I've seen...", "I tell teams..."
- Carries roughly a third of the words. The character carries the most.
- Plain, warm, specific.
- Gives his background once per listen, in the host intro that follows the open, and only when a background is given above (see the craft rules). Never invent one. Outside the intro, background comes up only as a short aside that backs up a point. Characterization only: it never carries a fact.
