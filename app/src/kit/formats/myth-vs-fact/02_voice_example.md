# Voice example

This is a sample of register for myth versus fact: how the hosts sound. It is a draft sample on a deliberately light topic (the fictional focaccia sandwich module), to be replaced with a hand-written one. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this sample's sequence of moves, its questions or its lines.

The open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Open (fixed shape)

DANA: Today we're checking what people say about slicing and building a focaccia sandwich against what the training says. What's true, what isn't, and what depends.
DANA: I'm Dana.
RAY: And I'm Ray.
DANA: Let's check some facts.

## Host intro (only when the expert has a background; same asset as the open)

DANA: Ray, you spent a long time in commercial kitchens before this, didn't you?
RAY: Twenty-some years, mostly on the line, then training new cooks.
DANA: Then you've seen every way a sandwich can go wrong.
RAY: Most of them, yes.
DANA: Good. Let's start.

## Close (fixed shape)

DANA: That's it for today. Thanks for listening!
RAY: Check the facts, trust the procedure, and we'll see you next time.

## Register sample

An excerpt from a topic part. Read it for how the hosts talk, not for what happens in it.

DANA: Ok, so here's one I've heard a hundred times: "Slice it while it's still warm, it cuts easier." Is that true?
RAY: Honestly, no. It feels easier, but warm focaccia squashes and the crust tears. The procedure says to cool it completely first.
DANA: Huh. I really thought I was being smart.
RAY: Most people do. It sounds like a good shortcut, which is why it sticks.
DANA: So if I'm in a rush, what do I do?
RAY: Plan for the cooling before the rush, so the bread's ready when you are.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- Plain, warm words. Full spoken sentences, with a few short reactions on their own line.
- The belief said once, in a worker's voice, and never repeated.
- A plain verdict in the first answer, then the reasons.
- Light improvisation ("honestly," "I mean") once or twice per part per speaker.
- Dana's questions build on what Ray just said.

## Not required

This sample happens to use certain moves: the myth in Dana's mouth, a verdict up front, Dana admitting she believed it, a one-line reason the myth sticks, a practical answer last. They are options, not a template. A part may use none of them. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Principles from review

- **Answers are full spoken sentences with a little improvisation.**
- **Each question builds on the answer before it.**
- **Never refer to something the listener hasn't heard yet.**

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure or a strength of evidence the claims ledger doesn't hold.**
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.**
- **Stock host lines** ("Any final thoughts?", "Solid advice") are fine once per listen, not in every part.
