## 1. The format

A host with experts. One host walks the listener through a topic and brings in a different expert voice in each part, the way a radio host plays tape from the people who know the job. Each expert answers one question, from their seat on the job. It is not a debate, a panel, or a lecture.

The experts are voices written for the show. In the audio they are only ever a generic role ("a safety manager," "a line operator," "a supervisor"), with employers kept generic. The audio never claims a real person was called, interviewed or recorded. The LMS may carry a note saying the voices are synthetic, if legal wants one. Write nothing about this in the script.

A module is a set of **parts**. The LMS assembles each learner's listen from those parts, based on a short quiz before the listen and checks after it.

Every listen: **open**, then the topic parts in a fixed order with a sting between them, then the **close**. There are no bridges, so each part's first lines do the job of moving to a new topic, and they do it as a spoken signpost that sounds like a person talking, never a label. The host brings the topic up conversationally, in a different way for each part. Moves to choose from, described and never to be copied as wording: turn to a co-speaker with a question that leads into the topic; react to something the previous part left behind, only if it can be said without referring to it; put a small everyday situation in front of the listener and ask what they would do; ask the co-speaker a plain "what is" or "why does" question; or state the plain stake in one sentence. Addressing a co-speaker by name is one option, used at most once per listen, never as a formula like "[name], let's talk about…". The host says what kind of thing comes next (a story, a question, a case) before any of it begins. Never open with a label such as "New topic:" or "Next up:". Any voice the listener hasn't heard yet is introduced by name and role right before it speaks, never mentioned and then cut to. The simulation follows, outside the audio. The audio never hands off to it.

## 2. The cast

**Dana (host)**
- Poses the question before any expert speaks, in plain narration of two to four sentences.
- Introduces the expert inside the part, by role only and with one line on what that role sees ("A safety manager sees the paperwork side of this."). Never a name, an employer, or how they were found.
- Genuinely doesn't know the answer. She is curious, not a straight man.
- Sums up what the last answer established in one sentence, then names the open question, or the other side, in her own words.
- Never plays two experts against each other. When she needs a contrast, she sets it up herself ("Supervisors tend to see this differently.").

**Expert A, Expert B and Expert C (expert voices)**
- Three distinct voices. Each part uses one of them. The beat sheet chooses the voice and the role, and a role is the credential: a safety manager, a line operator, a supervisor, a new hire. It is never a name.
- Their first words are about why this question matters on their job, not about their credentials.
- Speak from experience in plain, warm, specific sentences ("I've seen...", "On my line...").
- Carry most of the words, roughly 55 to 60 percent.
- Never mention another expert. Each answers one question.
- Their stories are characterization only. They never carry a fact, statistic, injury, exposure or outcome the claims ledger doesn't hold.
