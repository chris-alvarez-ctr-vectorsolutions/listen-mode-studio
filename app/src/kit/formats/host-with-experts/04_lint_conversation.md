## E. Host with experts

- [ ] The open names the topic and promises only content every learner hears, never names which experts will speak, and stays under 60 words.
- [ ] Each part has exactly one expert voice, introduced inside the part by role and one line on what that role sees.
- [ ] No expert is named, and no employer is named. Roles and employers are generic.
- [ ] Nothing says or implies a real person was called, interviewed or recorded ("I called up," "I spoke to," "we recorded").
- [ ] No expert mentions another expert. Any contrast is set up by Dana in her own words.
- [ ] Narration stays at two to four sentences between expert lines.
- [ ] Dana poses the question before the expert speaks, and sums up in one sentence before turning.
- [ ] The expert's first words are about why this matters on the job, not credentials.
- [ ] Every fact traces to the claims ledger. The expert's stories and what a role "sees" are characterization only.
- [ ] The part ends on the expert's own practical advice, or on a way out from the guide.
- [ ] No speaker has more than two lines in a row.
- [ ] Answers sound like speech: mostly full sentences with some varied rhythm. No bare lists of notes and no telegraphic fragments with no connective.
- [ ] Every question from Dana connects to the expert's previous answer.
- [ ] Nothing ("the shortcut," "that rule") is referred to before it's been introduced.
- [ ] No example wording from the production guide, lint checklist or pattern library appears in the script.
- [ ] No line repeats a line from the voice example, and no part follows its sequence of moves unless its beat sheet chose them.
- [ ] No two parts in a listen share the same overall shape.
- [ ] Read aloud, every line sounds like something a person would say out loud.
- [ ] At most one interruption per part, marked with dashes, never in a part about harm or inside a character's story, and never cutting off a ledger claim.
