# Voice example

This is a sample of register for a host with experts: how the voices sound. It is a draft sample on a deliberately light topic (the fictional focaccia sandwich module), to be replaced with a hand-written one. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this sample's sequence of moves, its questions or its lines.

The open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Open (fixed shape)

DANA: Today we're talking about slicing and building a focaccia sandwich, and how it looks from different seats in the kitchen.
DANA: I'm Dana. Let's get into it.

## Close (fixed shape)

DANA: That's it for today. Thank you for listening, and we'll be back soon.

## Register sample

An excerpt from a topic part. Read it for how the voices talk, not for what happens in it.

DANA: Here's something I never thought about: does it matter how long the bread sits before you slice it? A prep supervisor sees this one all day.
EXPERT A: Yeah, honestly it's the whole thing. The procedure says cool it completely first. Slice it warm and the crumb squashes and the crust tears.
DANA: So the shortcut is the problem, not the knife.
EXPERT A: Pretty much. Plan the cooling before the rush, and the rush takes care of itself.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- Plain, warm words. Full spoken sentences, with a few short reactions on their own line.
- The host poses the question, then names the role in one line.
- The expert opens with why it matters on the job, not with credentials.
- The host sums up in a sentence, then turns.
- Nobody claims the expert was called, interviewed or recorded.

## Not required

This sample happens to use certain moves: a question first, a role named in a line, a one-sentence summary, a practical close. They are options, not a template. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Principles from review

- **Answers are full spoken sentences with a little improvisation.**
- **Each question builds on the answer before it.**
- **Never refer to something the listener hasn't heard yet.**

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure the claims ledger doesn't hold.**
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.** It never names which experts will speak.
- **Never say or imply a real person was called, interviewed or recorded.**
