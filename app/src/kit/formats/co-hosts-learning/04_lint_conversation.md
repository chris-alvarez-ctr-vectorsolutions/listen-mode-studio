## D. Uncanny-valley lint

| Check | Starting target |
| --- | --- |
| Each co-host's share of words in a part | Between about 40 and 60 percent |
| Restatements per part | One at most |
| Facts attributed to a source | Every one |
| Each way in and way out | Used once per listen |
| "Solid advice," "Any final thoughts?" and other stock host lines | Once per listen each |
| Turn length | Mixed: fragments, short and long turns. Not uniform |
| Concrete, visual details per part | At least one |
| Light or funny moments per part | One at most |
| CAPS emphasis per part | One or two words |

## E. Co-hosts learning

- [ ] The open names the topic, promises only content every learner hears, says the co-hosts are learning together, has no scene, no character and no host background, and stays under 60 words.
- [ ] Every fact is attributed to its source in plain words, and any source named is one the claims ledger holds.
- [ ] Each co-host brings different parts of the material, and neither becomes the expert.
- [ ] Neither co-host claims a career, years of experience or authority ("in my years," "I've seen," "trust me").
- [ ] Anything the material doesn't cover is said plainly and listed as a gap for the SME. Nothing is guessed, and no joke states or implies a fact.
- [ ] Each part's first answer defines the topic in plain words, and the other co-host connects it to work.
- [ ] Each co-host's line about their own work is characterization only and carries no fact, statistic, injury or outcome.
- [ ] The part ends with the practical list said once (if the ledger holds one), then a short sign-off.
- [ ] Answers sound like speech: mostly full sentences with some varied rhythm. No bare lists of notes and no telegraphic fragments with no connective.
- [ ] Every question connects to the answer before it, and jargon is explained in the next line.
- [ ] Nothing ("the shortcut," "that rule") is referred to before it's been introduced.
- [ ] No example wording from the production guide, lint checklist or pattern library appears in the script.
- [ ] No line repeats a line from the voice example, and no part follows its sequence of moves unless its beat sheet chose them.
- [ ] No two parts in a listen share the same overall shape.
- [ ] Read aloud, every line sounds like something a person would say out loud.
- [ ] At most one interruption per part, marked with dashes, never in a part about harm or inside a character's story, and never cutting off a ledger claim.
- [ ] No standalone one-word reaction turn. Reactions ride on the front of the same speaker's next line.
- [ ] Dana has turns other than questions (a reaction, a scenario she sets up, what she'd do differently). A wrong assumption she voices is corrected in full in the next turn and is backed by the ledger.
- [ ] No fact, list or statistic inside a character's story. The facts follow the story.
- [ ] No "before the show" or "off mic" setup, no host thanking another for explaining, no canned personality line.
- [ ] Every expert turn answers a question or invitation the host aimed at him in the turn before. No question is left in the air for him to pick up.
