# Voice example

This is a sample of register for two co-hosts learning together: how the voices sound. It is a draft sample on a deliberately light topic (the fictional focaccia sandwich module), to be replaced with a hand-written one. How a part is built comes from its beat sheet, the pattern library and the production guide. Do not reuse this sample's sequence of moves, its questions or its lines.

The open and the close are the one place the structure is fixed. Match their shape.

## Open (fixed shape)

DANA: Today we're learning how to build a focaccia sandwich, and we're figuring it out together.
DANA: I'm Dana.
RAY: And I'm Ray.
DANA: Let's dig in.

## Close (fixed shape)

DANA: That's it for today. Thanks for listening!
RAY: Keep learning, and we'll be back soon.

## Register sample

An excerpt from a topic part. Read it for how the voices talk, not for what happens in it.

DANA: Ok, so I read the section on slicing. The procedure says to cool the focaccia completely before you slice it.
RAY: Huh. I'd have sliced it warm, honestly. Easier to cut, right?
DANA: That's what I figured too. But the training says slicing it warm squashes the crumb and tears the crust.
RAY: Ok, that's good to know. What I read is about the knife. A serrated one, with a gentle sawing motion instead of pressing down.
DANA: Does it say how long it takes to cool?
RAY: It doesn't. The training doesn't give a time, so we'd check with whoever runs the kitchen.

## Register

This is what to take from the sample.

- Plain, warm words. Full spoken sentences, with a few short reactions on their own line.
- Each fact attributed to its source in plain words.
- Each co-host brings a different part of the material.
- An honest "it doesn't say" in place of a guess.
- Neither sounds like the expert. Both sound like the learner.

## Not required

This sample happens to use certain moves: one host reports, the other reacts, a split in who reads what, and a plain gap at the end. They are options, not a template. The pattern library lists the shapes a part can take, and each part picks its own from its beat sheet.

## Principles from review

- **Answers are full spoken sentences with a little improvisation.**
- **Each question builds on the answer before it.**
- **Never refer to something the listener hasn't heard yet.**

## What not to copy

- **Never state a figure the claims ledger doesn't hold,** and never guess at a fact, even as a joke.
- **Name a source only as the ledger names it.**
- **The open promises only what every learner hears.**
- **Stock host lines** ("Any final thoughts?", "Solid advice") are fine once per listen, not in every part.
