# Pattern library

Techniques from mainstream podcasts, described in our own words. Use the techniques. Never imitate a specific host's voice or catchphrases.

These shape the topic parts, not the cold open. The cold open always follows the voice example.

## What all of them share

- They open on something specific (an email, one sentence, a strange news item), never the category.
- People come in through a story. Credentials take one line, usually in the guest's own words.
- The host doesn't know everything, and says so.
- Jargon gets explained the moment it's said.
- The seams between segments are signposted: a tease before, a re-anchor after.

## By show

**The Economics of Everyday Things** (closest to our format)
- One host, one expert.
- Opens on a strange, concrete news item, then widens to the real scope, then names the topic in two words.
- The expert enters as "someone like" a named person, then states his own experience.
- One running example is carried through a whole process, step by step.
- Closes on a personal anecdote from the expert that lands the point about shared responsibility.
- *For us:* follow one blade or one needle through the whole part. End on something Ray saw happen.

**Ologies**
- The host is a curious non-expert who asks the obvious questions out loud.
- A quick run of the questions the episode will answer, before it answers any.
- The guest's first question is how they got into the field.
- *For us:* Dana asks the question the listener is embarrassed to ask. Ray's background comes through a story, not a résumé.

**Freakonomics Radio**
- Opens on a human truth, then a surprising concrete hook.
- Previews the voices to come, each labeled by role.
- Explains a term in plain words right after the guest uses it.
- Teases a break with a question: "Can you guess which one?"
- *For us:* when Ray uses a term like "engineering control," Dana or Ray explains it in the next line.

**Radiolab**
- Names the topic as one countable, concrete thing.
- Tape first, name after: you hear the person explain before you learn who they are.
- One host admits he doesn't understand; the other pushes back.
- Re-anchors the premise after every break.
- *For us:* each part is about one concrete thing. Dana's not-knowing is real.

**Search Engine**
- Starts inside a real person's question, with no welcome first.
- Sets the stakes fast: who this matters to and why now.
- Texture before stakes: small, funny, human details before the point.
- *For us:* a part can start on the question a worker would actually ask.

**Hidden Brain**
- Show ID first, then a familiar reference, then questions aimed at the listener.
- The research comes after the guest's personal story.
- Listener voice memos serve as evidence.
- *For us:* questions to the listener ("Out of ten people on your shift...") before the answer.
